### Data traps to avoid in the deck
1. **Don't use RBI Annual Report "card/internet/digital" fraud numbers to size scams.** The FY26 figure of 293 cases / ₹29 crore is a reporting artefact. The table covers only frauds of ₹1 lakh and above, and since the July 2024 Master Directions only frauds "concluded as frauds on the bank" are reported. Customer-authorised (APP) scams mostly drop out of it. Use NCRP/I4C numbers (#1-#7) to size scams.
2. **Three different "2025 loss" figures are in circulation:**
   - ₹22,495 crore across 28.15 lakh complaints: MHA data via ThePrint.
   - ₹22,931 crore across 28 lakh: NCRP "digital payment frauds", as quoted in RBI's paper.
   - ₹19,812-19,813 crore across 21.7 lakh complaints: McAfee and ThePrint social posts, probably a different cut-off date or category.

   Pick one and footnote it. **Recommended:** "₹22,495 crore lost to cyber-fraud in 2025 (MHA/NCRP), 76% of it to investment/trading scams."
3. **Bank-reported UPI fraud is small next to NCRP losses.** UPI fraud was ₹981 crore in FY25, against about ₹22,800 crore of NCRP losses. Most money lost to scams moves in high-value, customer-authorised transfers (98.5% of value is in transactions above ₹10k). That is the authorised-push-payment (APP) problem, not "unauthorised UPI fraud".
4. **Conflict on digital-arrest trend [U].** A LinkedIn post citing the Supreme Court's 4 Aug 2026 order says digital-arrest complaints fell from 1.23 lakh (2024) to "under 16,400". ThePrint's "6% of 28.15 lakh cases" works out to about 1.7 lakh cases in 2025. These don't agree. Don't quote either without checking the Supreme Court order.
5. **No 2026 year-to-date NCRP loss figure was found.** The latest official cumulative figures run to 30 Jun 2026 (rows #6-#9).

## 2. Job A detail notes

### RBI
- **RBI Annual Report 2025-26, Chapter VI** (https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/AnnualReportPublications.aspx?Id=1466):
  - Fraud trends by bank group:
    - Public sector banks: 5,418 cases / ₹35,709 cr (74.5% of value).
    - Private banks: 3,956 cases / ₹11,399 cr.
  - "While number of frauds was highest under card/internet/digital payments category during 2023-24 and 2024-25, advances category accounted for the largest share in 2025-26."
  - **MuleHunter.ai (para VI.57).** It assigns each account a confidence score (a probability that it is a mule) using "behavioural and transactional features derived from real-world fraud patterns". It is live in 26 banks as of 31 Mar 2026. "The course of action ... rests with the individual banks."
  - **DPIP (para VI.66).** Phase 1 is a smart registry of suspicious/fraudulent actors, so they can't be onboarded. Phase 2 is an AI/ML **real-time, transaction-by-transaction risk score** for banks. 7 banks are in the pilot.
  - **Draft revision of customer-liability rules (para VI.48, issued 6 Mar 2026).** It would widen the limited-liability rules to cover more types of fraudulent e-banking transactions, shorten complaint timelines, and add **compensation for small-value frauds**.
  - Box VI.3 lists bank.in, MuleHunter.ai, the HaRBInger innovation programme, the regulatory sandbox, and awareness campaigns on digital arrest, 1930 and renting out bank accounts.
- **RBI Discussion Paper (9 Apr 2026; comments closed 8 May 2026)**, https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?id=23810. This is the most important 2026 policy signal for Track 02. Proposals:
  1. **A one-hour lag on authorised push payments above ₹10,000.** The bank debits provisionally and the payer can cancel for any reason during the hour. This applies to individuals, sole proprietors and partnerships.
  2. **Confirmation by a trusted person for transactions above ₹50,000.** Mandatory for people aged 70+ and persons with disabilities; optional for everyone else.
  3. **"Shadow credit" on low-turnover accounts.** If annual credits go above ₹25 lakh without supporting proof, the funds can't be used until the bank verifies them.
  4. **Customer controls.** An on/off switch and limits for each payment mode, plus a one-tap **"kill switch"** that disables all digital payments.

  The paper also cites international models: the UK's 72-hour hold, Singapore's 12-hour cooling-off plus kill switch, Sweden's trusted person, Australia's digital padlock. It names "bogus call centres, deepfake-driven impersonation scams and mule account networks".
- **RBI Authentication Directions, 2025** (RBI/2025-26/79, 25 Sep 2025; effective 1 Apr 2026), https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=12898:
  - Minimum two-factor authentication, with one factor dynamic for non-card-present transactions.
  - **Sec. 8, Risk-based approach.** Issuers "may ... evaluate against behavioural / contextual parameters such as transaction location, user behaviour patterns, device attributes, historical transaction profile ... additional checks beyond the minimum two-factor authentication may be resorted to. Issuers may also explore using DigiLocker as a platform for notification and confirmation for high-risk transactions."
  - Cross-border card-not-present validation is due by 1 Oct 2026.
- **bank.in domain.** RBI required banks to migrate to bank.in by 31 Oct 2025, and major banks completed it (ETBFSI: https://bfsi.economictimes.indiatimes.com/articles/indian-banks-transition-to-bankin-domain-to-combat-digital-fraud/125578668). A "fin.in" domain for other financial entities is also reported [U on status].
- **Draft KYC Amendment Directions, 2026 (mid-Sep 2026)** (ET, 14 Sep 2026: https://m.economictimes.com/wealth/save/rbi-proposes-60-day-temporary-debit-hold-on-suspicious-money-mule-transactions-key-things-you-need-to-know-about-rbis-kyc-directions/articleshow/134241278.cms):
  - Banks can place a **temporary debit hold of up to 60 days** on a suspected mule transaction (₹1,000 and above) or on the whole account.
  - The customer must be notified, with reasons and a contact officer, and gets **20 days** to explain.
  - The hold is removed on the 31st day after reference to law enforcement if no contrary instruction arrives.
  - This follows the **Supreme Court's 4 Aug 2026 order** (and an earlier 9 Feb 2026 order) asking RBI for a mule-account SOP (LawBeat: https://lawbeat.in/columns/digital-arrest-scams-the-hard-questions-behind-the-supreme-courts-new-directions-1625775).

### I4C / MHA (see table rows 1-12)
- **Flow is still the main weakness.** Over FY24-26, ₹56,087 cr was reported, ₹9,079 cr was lien-marked, and only ₹206 cr was refunded. A Money Restoration Module and a Grievance Redressal Module (for wrongly frozen accounts) went live in April 2026, under an MHA SOP dated 2 Jan 2026 (PIB 2287674).
- **Coordination infrastructure:**
  - CFMC: banks, payment players, telecoms, IT platforms and police working together at I4C.
  - Samanvaya: shared crime data and analytics for police, which has led to 29,837 arrests.
  - Sahyog portal: takedown notices to IT intermediaries.
  - Suspect Registry, launched 10 Sep 2024.
  - JCCTs (joint cyber coordination teams) for Mewat, Jamtara, Ahmedabad, Hyderabad, Chandigarh, Visakhapatnam and Guwahati.
- **I4C-RBIH MoU (12 May 2026)** (PIB: https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2260277). Suspect Registry data will be fed to MuleHunter.ai, and RBIH will use it to train and improve its fraud-risk models.

### NPCI / UPI
- Monthly data is in table row 14. August 2026 had the highest volume (24.51 bn). September 2026 dipped 1.8% to 24.07 bn / ₹29.37 lakh cr.
- **NPCI fraud controls:**
  - A fraud-monitoring service for all banks that uses AI/ML models to raise alerts and decline transactions (MoF reply, Dec 2025, via IANS).
  - Device binding, PIN-based second factor, daily limits.
  - **P2P "collect" requests were discontinued from 1 Oct 2025** (NPCI circular of 29 Jul 2025; TOI: https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/business/india-business/upi-fraud-clampdown-npci-to-end-p2p-collect-requests-from-october-1-banks-apps-told-to-block-pull-transactions-permanently/articleshow/123309612.cms).
  - Comprehensive UPI Information Security Framework 2025 and a Mobile Application Security Framework (PIB 2286608).
  - NPCI has also told UPI apps to mask users' mobile numbers [S, Fortune India social post].
- **UPI Help / MyUPI** (https://upihelp.npci.org.in/): status checks, complaints and mandate management. It is a support tool after the payment, not a warning before it.

### Surveys on AI voice clones and deepfakes (table rows 22-24)
- The best recent India stat is McAfee, Feb 2026: "1 in 5 Indians have encountered a voice-clone scam", "4 deepfakes/day". For a stronger headline, McAfee 2023 has "47% experienced or know a victim of an AI voice scam; 83% of victims lost money".

**What this means.** The obvious Track 02 pitches are already built at national scale:
- "Flag risky UPI IDs or phone numbers" is FRI plus the Suspect Registry.
- "Detect mule accounts" is MuleHunter.ai.
- "AI scam-SMS/link checker" is Truecaller Scam Checker, Chakshu and Google Messages.
- "Warn when screen-sharing during a call" is Google's pilot.
- "Real-time transaction risk score" is DPIP Phase 2 and NPCI's monitoring.

A student deck that re-pitches any of these will score low on differentiation (15 points: "more than a generic AI detection claim") unless it explicitly builds on top of them.

### 3.2 Gaps a student product could still own
1. **Detection across the whole scam journey, not one transaction.** Every incumbent scores a single entity: a number (FRI), an account (MuleHunter) or a transaction (NPCI/DPIP). Big-ticket scams unfold over hours or days in recognisable stages:
   - Investment/trading scam: WhatsApp/Telegram group, then a fake app, then a small "profit" withdrawal, then escalating transfers to new payees. This is 76% of losses.
   - Task-based scam: small credits come in, then escalating "deposits" go out.
   - Digital arrest: a long call or video call, then the victim breaks FDs, then multiple RTGS/IMPS transfers to new payees.

   A model that recognises the **stage** (for example, "3rd escalating transfer to a new payee within 48 hours after receiving small credits") and steps in **before the big transfer** is new. It also fits the facts: 98.5% of value is in transactions above ₹10k, and only 0.4% of reported money is ever refunded.
2. **Alert-fatigue math as a design centrepiece.**
   - At about 7 frauds per million UPI transactions (FY25), a classifier that is 99.9% accurate on legitimate payments still raises about 1,000 false alerts for every 7 real frauds.
   - The answer is **context-gated, tiered intervention**: no alert for low-value, known payees. A nudge only when several independent signals line up (new payee + high value + an FRI flag or a known scam stage + an unknown caller on the line). Friction or a cool-off only at the top tier. A trusted person only when the user is vulnerable or has opted in.
   - Show precision/recall on a **synthetic scam-journey dataset** and an "alert budget" (for example, at most one interruption per user per month). Reviewers are explicitly scoring this.
3. **Be the decision engine for RBI's proposed controls.** RBI is proposing blunt rules (a lag on every APP above ₹10k; trusted-person approval above ₹50k for 70+). A risk engine that decides **when** to apply the one-hour lag, the trusted-person step or a DigiLocker confirmation (as Sec. 8 of the Authentication Directions allows) reduces friction for the roughly 99.99% of genuine payments. That is a clear before-and-after story and an obvious owner: a bank or app's trust-and-safety team.
4. **A consented "trusted person / family guardian" flow for seniors and first-time users.** RBI has proposed it, but no large UPI app ships it that I could find [U]. It is privacy-sensitive, so build it on consent and share only the minimum: "Dad is about to send ₹2,00,000 to a new payee flagged High risk; call him", with no transaction history.
5. **Explanations in Indian languages tied to the specific scam script.** Incumbents show generic warnings. Name the pattern: "This looks like a 'digital arrest' scam. Police and CBI never ask you to transfer money to 'verify' it." That is better user value, and the brief says "a score alone is not an explanation".
6. **Golden-hour evidence pack.** If a scam completes, auto-assemble a 1930/NCRP-ready bundle (UTRs, payee VPAs/accounts, timestamps, caller numbers, screenshots) and one-tap "call 1930 now" plus "freeze via kill switch". The figures show the leak is speed and completeness: ₹56,087 cr reported, ₹9,079 cr liened, ₹206 cr refunded.
7. **Contestability and accountability.** The ET Government analysis (10 Sep 2026) criticises FRI for opacity, false positives that spread across banks, and no clear review or restore path. A design with auditable rules, reason codes, and an appeal path for wrongly flagged people (merchants and gig workers who receive many small credits) speaks directly to the 15-point safety criterion.
8. **Adoption angle.** Amazon is the sponsor, and Amazon Pay is a UPI app. Pitch the operating owner as a "PSP/bank trust-and-safety team" and integrate through existing signals (FRI on DIP, the I4C Suspect Registry, NPCI fraud monitoring). Do not invent new data collection. This assumes you can't access real data; simulate with synthetic journeys, as the brief requires.

### 3.3 What NOT to pitch in Track 02 (reviewers will have seen it 50+ times)
- "XGBoost/Random Forest on PaySim or the Kaggle credit-card dataset, as a UPI fraud detector with a dashboard." GitHub, YouTube tutorials, IJRASET papers and Devpost entries are full of "UPI Fraud Detection using ML" (for example https://github.com/Vatshayan/UPI-Fraud-Detection-Using-Machine-Learning, https://www.ijraset.com/research-paper/ai-powered-upi-fraud-detection-and-alert-system, https://devpost.com/software/upi_fraud_detection).
- "LLM that classifies scam SMS or links" (now commoditised by Truecaller Scam Checker as of 28 Sep 2026).
- "Blacklist of fraud UPI IDs" (this is FRI and the Suspect Registry).
- "Auto-block suspicious transactions" (the brief explicitly says to avoid automatic blocking on one weak signal).

## 4. Crowding assessment (my opinion, not data)

Assumption: a few hundred student teams, many drafting with AI assistants. I don't know whether finalist slots are allocated per track; **ask the organisers.** If the top 30 are chosen overall, crowding mainly hurts *differentiation*. If slots are per track, it also hurts *odds*.

| Track | My estimated share of submissions | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **02 Scam Pattern Recognition** | **About 40-50% (most crowded)** | (a) "UPI fraud detection" is the default Indian student ML project, with ready datasets, tutorials and repos. (b) AI assistants given "AI cybersecurity + India + payments" land on UPI scam detection first. (c) Easiest to demo (tabular model plus an SMS classifier plus a dashboard), and needs no audio or video ML. (d) The name sounds the broadest. Most of these decks will look alike, so a well-differentiated "scam-journey + alert-budget + RBI-control decision engine" deck stands out to reviewers, but the median entry will be generic. |
| **03 Deepfake Detection for Financial Communications** | **About 25-35%** | Deepfake detection is a popular CV/ML topic with public datasets (FaceForensics++, DFDC) and pretrained HuggingFace detectors, so an "upload a video, get real/fake" demo is easy. Many teams will do generic media forensics and under-deliver on the "financial communications" and "actionable review/reporting path" parts. The differentiated angle is provenance and verification (fake RBI/SEBI notices, fake bank letters or KYC videos, deepfake celebrity investment ads, which feed the investment-scam category that is 76% of losses). That is less crowded than pure detection. Detection accuracy claims are fragile: a WEF-cited study reports 45-50% accuracy drops on real-world deepfakes. |
| **01 Voice Cloning Fraud Detection** | **About 20-25% (least crowded)** | Real-time audio is hard to build in 48 hours. Telephony integration, call-recording consent and Indian-language data (ASVspoof is mostly English) put teams off, and incumbents (Pindrop, Resemble, Google on-device) look intimidating. But it has the strongest headline stats for India (McAfee: 47% / 83% / 1 in 5), and safety design (consent, on-device processing, no auto-block, a callback-verification path) can earn many of the 15 safety points. Risk: detector accuracy on phone-quality, codec-compressed Hindi/regional audio is uncertain. A credible deck should rely on **call-context and behavioural signals plus out-of-band verification** (for example, "a family member or bank official is asking for money: verify via a pre-agreed safe word or a callback"), not on the acoustic detector alone. |

**Bottom line.**
- Track 02 is very likely the most crowded, and its obvious ideas are already national infrastructure (FRI, MuleHunter.ai, Suspect Registry, NPCI monitoring, Truecaller and Google warnings).
- It is still winnable if the deck is explicitly *a layer on top of FRI, MuleHunter and the Suspect Registry*. That means detecting the multi-step scam journey, budgeting alerts, and choosing when to trigger RBI's proposed controls (lag, trusted person, kill switch).
- Tracks 01 and 03 are less crowded. A deck there wins by being honest about detector limits and routing high-risk moments to out-of-band verification and the 1930/NCRP path.
