# Make verification the product, detection the clue

Pehchaan should pitch itself as the verification-and-decision layer for the moment a UPI payer is about to send money during or just after a call. It catches the cloned-voice **attack** at Pay by checking the claim behind the payment with the real person, and it treats every voice detector, its own included, as a capped, replaceable clue. That framing turns the track's main scoring risk, judges who expect a detector score, into the organisers' own thesis. The declared evidence already supports it: the Round 1 detector went from **4.8% EER on simulated channels to 24.6% on real calls**, and model A, retrained to 6.8% on the training handset, still caught only **43% of held-out Chatterbox clones** on real calls ([FINDINGS.md at e6370a5](https://github.com/Galabavamsi/pehchaan/blob/e6370a5/ml/realcall/FINDINGS.md)). Under Option 2, every result from 20–23 Oct stays off the slides, so four claims must be re-earned on site from a branch cut at e6370a5: the live two-phone attack stopped at Pay, a fresh held-out-handset test, a fresh new-cloner test and a comprehension test. The 23 Oct deck cites undeclared numbers and must be rebuilt from the declared source. On one A10G in about 1.5 days, detector gains come from scoring, calibration, starting weights and data, not new architectures; the most distinctive addition is a few-shot adapter of the kind that cut ElevenLabs EER from 40.31% zero-shot to 3.31% with 20 clips without forgetting older generators ([ADD-GP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23619)). Four new signals clear the bar of shipped precedent, quick mockability and independence from acoustics: guardian co-approval, CNAP caller-name mismatch, screen sharing during a call and a human-in-the-loop challenge. Speaker verification and watermarking do not. India's national stack can tell whether a number or account is bad, and PhonePe and Google warn people who pay while on a call, but no Indian product checks with the real person who asked for the payment. The problem slide should lead with four primary numbers and say plainly that India does not count voice-clone losses separately: **₹22,931 crore lost across 28 lakh NCRP digital-payment frauds in 2025**, **19,564 Chakshu reports of relative or friend impersonation**, **₹206 crore refunded of ₹56,087 crore reported (0.37%)** and **98.5% of fraud value in payments above ₹10,000**.

## Judges should see the attack stopped at Pay, not a detector score

RAKSHAM scores the finale out of 100: **problem fit 20, architecture and detection logic 20, value and differentiation 15, feasibility and finale plan 15, safety, privacy and responsible AI 15, adoption and integration 10, and communication 5**. Its brief asks teams to avoid automatic blocking or accusation on a single weak signal, to put human review before any high-impact action, and to plan for fraudsters adapting. It also says that "a detector score alone is not an explanation or proof" ([Unstop listing](https://unstop.com/competitions/raksham-ai-cybersecurity-hackathon-iit-delhi-1761331)). That is Pehchaan's design in the organisers' own words, and it belongs on a slide.

The exposed flank is the track title, Voice Cloning Fraud Detection. Judges who anchor on classifier accuracy will compare Pehchaan's honest real-call numbers with rivals' clean-test scores. The defence is to define what Pehchaan detects as the attack itself, a cloned or fake caller asking for money intercepted at the payment, and to show the voice detector as one capped input to an explainable decision. The Round 1 brief asked reviewers whether they could name the user, the harmful moment and the threat pattern within a minute. Pehchaan's one-sentence answer: it protects UPI payers, elders first, at the moment they pay a new payee during or within 10 minutes of a call, when a cloned relative or fake bank caller is pressing for urgent money.

Judging practice points the same way. One judge's notes from a year of hackathons report several patterns. A live demo removes doubt, and narrow scope done well beats platform ambition. Naming a weakness before being asked raised scores, the opening 30 seconds largely set the score, and blurring earlier work into event work reads as concealment ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/kurbaitaev/what-judges-actually-score-notes-from-a-year-of-hackathon-judging-3p4l)).

The pitch should therefore open on two real phones. A consented clone of the "son" calls and the payer taps Pay. Pehchaan asks "Who asked you to pay?", and Family Verify reaches the real son's phone. He taps "Not me" and the payment is held. The same flow with the real son then goes through with one tap. Only after that should the deck explain why detectors cannot hold money and how the fusion works.

Every weakness the team volunteers needs a design response attached, or it reads as failure. For example: the retrained model still misses held-out generators on real calls, so voice runs in shadow mode and can never hold money (guardrail G2). Four questions are near certain, and each has an answer built on declared evidence.

The first is detector credibility. The team's own retrained model caught 43% of Chatterbox and 62% of Seed-VC clones on real calls ([FINDINGS.md at e6370a5](https://github.com/Galabavamsi/pehchaan/blob/e6370a5/ml/realcall/FINDINGS.md)). ElevenLabs says its own classifier does not reliably classify its newest v3 model ([ElevenLabs](https://elevenlabs.io/ai-speech-classifier)). Open-source detectors lost 48% AUC on in-the-wild audio from 88 websites ([Deepfake-Eval-2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02857)). Pindrop's and Hiya's claims of around 99% are self-reported, and the research found no audit on Indian-language speakerphone audio ([Pindrop](https://www.pindrop.com/resources/article/pindrop-pulse-for-audio-deepfake-detection); [Hiya](https://www.hiya.com/products/ai-voice)). A bank that prefers a vendor can plug it into Pehchaan's voice slot.

The second is coached victims. The declared ablation projects that call context alone puts **85.303% of simulated scams at Amber or above** ([deck_numbers.json at e6370a5](https://github.com/Galabavamsi/pehchaan/blob/e6370a5/engine/results/deck_numbers.json)). UK ombudsman decisions show scammer-coached customers choosing false payment purposes ([FOS DRN-5249672](https://www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk/decision/DRN-5249672.pdf)), which is why Pehchaan checks the payer's answer instead of trusting it.

The third is feasibility, and the honest answer names the dependencies. Every national and bank adapter is mocked. Google Play restricts call-log access to apps whose core function needs it ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9047303)). Detecting WhatsApp calls is unverified, and iOS is more restrictive. The team should confirm Family Verify rate limiting and device-key storage in code before claiming either.

The fourth is privacy. The family graph is personal data under India's DPDP Rules, whose consent and notice obligations apply from 13 May 2027 ([Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/indias-digital-personal-data-protection-act-2023-brought-into-force-)).

For Amazon judges, note that Connect Voice ID reached end of support on 20 May 2026, and AWS pointed customers to partners such as Pindrop or to one-time passcodes ([AWS](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/connect/latest/adminguide/amazonconnect-voiceid-end-of-support.html)). Pehchaan's bank-registry check needs no voiceprint, and a contact centre's outbound-call records could feed it. Amazon Pay's UPI Circle already models a family graph ([Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.in/news/retail/amazon-pay-upi-circle-family-payments)). The research found no public in-call scam control from Amazon Pay, so the safe phrasing is that no public feature was found.

## One A10G buys calibration, better starting weights and a new-cloner adapter

The 2025–26 literature agrees that the encoder's pretraining and the training recipe matter far more than the classifier head. Amphion's Teffic-Audio report ranks first on the 14-set Speech-DF-Arena at **1.454% pooled EER** with a w2v-BERT 2.0 encoder, but its weights are proprietary. Its ablation shows each recipe step lowering pooled EER: attack- and source-balanced sampling from 7.159% to 6.456%, a supplementary genuine-speech corpus to 5.435%, and diverse augmentation to 1.454%. The same recipe lifts an XLS-R 300M encoder to 6.079%, against 16.079% for the leaderboard's XLSR+SLS entry ([Teffic-Audio](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28351)).

Other levers move little. Newer heads such as Nes2Net and Mamba variants gain roughly one to two EER points on In-the-Wild, all trained on English ASVspoof 2019 and none tested on telephony ([Nes2Net](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05657)). A 113-run study found that no auxiliary loss keeps an advantage over plain cross-entropy across architectures, corpora and seeds, and that single-seed rankings reverse ([Collapse, Not Invariance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00539)). Sharpness-aware minimisation moved an XLS-R linear model's In-the-Wild EER from 6.00% to 5.99% ([SAM study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11532)). Seed variance is large: one XLS-R configuration spans 15.0–32.9% ASVspoof 5 EER across seeds ([Is Broader Better?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29138)). Every on-site comparison is single-seed and must say so.

Real phone channels are where detectors break, and the newest evidence matches Pehchaan's own results. One study injected or played cloned speech through real calls on two Android handsets. That raised detection by 39% in the lab and 57% on a held-out real-world benchmark. Every system was scored at 8 kHz with voice activity detection, and decisions were averaged at checkpoints from 2 to 15 seconds of net speech. Loudspeaker playback was harder than direct injection ([It's All in the Presentation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26471)).

RealComm, posted on 20 Oct 2026, found that call transmission degrades every detector it tested, that acoustic injection is hardest, and that bandwidth loss alone does not explain the difficulty. Simulated augmentation plus real-call training gave the lowest call-domain error for all three detectors; Teffic-Audio fell from 37.94% to 22.37% ([RealComm](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27459)). Pehchaan's declared ablation has the same shape. Adding real-call clips alone gave **9.3%** real-call EER, adding the learned speakerphone channel alone gave **18.6%**, and adding both gave **6.8%** ([FINDINGS.md at e6370a5](https://github.com/Galabavamsi/pehchaan/blob/e6370a5/ml/realcall/FINDINGS.md)). That parallel belongs on the architecture slide, with its single-seed, single-handset caveat.

Starting weights are the cheapest lever left. NII's AntiDeepfake models were post-trained on 56,000 hours of genuine and 18,000 hours of artefact speech in more than 100 languages. After fine-tuning on Deepfake-Eval-2024, post-training cut 4-second EER from 19.96% to 11.86% for XLS-R-1B and from 19.77% to 17.15% for MMS-300M. The largest gains came on short inputs ([AntiDeepfake](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21090)). The weights are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, fine for a demo but not for a bank product ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/nii-yamagishilab/mms-300m-anti-deepfake)).

The team's declared baselines qualify that promise ([SUMMARY.md at e6370a5](https://github.com/Galabavamsi/pehchaan/blob/e6370a5/ml/results/SUMMARY.md)). Off the shelf, NII MMS-300M reached 15.1% EER against v1's 4.8% on simulated channels. It generalised better to the held-out speaker S02 (10.3% against 16.6%). But it scored 30.7% on simulated speaker-to-microphone replay, and it flagged 95.7% and 100% of genuine chunks on the Round 1 dialer recording. NII adds speaker generalisation, not channel robustness. Fusion and re-initialisation are therefore hypotheses to test on the real-call dev split, not guaranteed wins, even though fusing detectors built on different pretrained encoders cut ASVspoof 5 EER from 4.08% to 2.71% in published work ([arXiv 2501.05545](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.05545)).

New commercial cloners need a different tool. On SEA-Spoof, a strong detector's zero-shot EER was 61.40% on commercial generators and 65.50% on ElevenLabs. Fine-tuning on the new data brought Hindi commercial clones to 0.06%, but it raised the model's ASVspoof 5 EER from 1.25% to 5.6%: the model forgot old attacks ([SEA-Spoof](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19865)). ADD-GP avoids that trade. It freezes an XLS-R encoder and adds new clips to a Gaussian-process reference set. ElevenLabs EER fell from 40.31% zero-shot to 7.89% with 5 clips, 3.31% with 20 and 0.54% with 100, while EER on the original generators stayed at 0.1% ([ADD-GP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23619)).

Those results come from clean English read speech, and the code release is unconfirmed. The on-site version is therefore a minimal Gaussian-process or nearest-neighbour classifier on frozen embeddings, with logistic calibration. It also gives a clean operations story: flag an unfamiliar cloner, collect consented clips, refit the reference set and re-check old-generator EER. Sarvam released instant voice cloning in its SDKs on 12 Oct 2026, twelve days before the finale ([Sarvam changelog](https://docs.sarvam.ai/changelog/2026/10/12)), which makes the drill concrete.

Two more findings shape the scoring code. EER falls steadily with speech duration: XLS-R-1B on Deepfake-Eval-2024 scores 11.86% at 4 s, 10.00% at 10 s and 8.28% at 50 s ([AntiDeepfake](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21090)). Scoring overlapping 4-second windows across the call and averaging log-odds is therefore a gain that needs only code. English-trained detectors also collapse on Indian languages: AASIST rose from 10.13% to 40.32% EER zero-shot on Indic-CodecFake ([Indic-CodecFake](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19949)). The research found no public Indian real-phone-call deepfake benchmark, which makes Pehchaan's consented Jio call set and pre-registered protocol a contribution worth naming.

## Four new signals pass the precedent, mockability and voice-independence test

SPEC v0.2 already has six signal families: the claim; call context, including VoIP, the 1600 series and DoT FRI; payment context; voice, in shadow mode; script cues, by tap checklist or ASR; and decisive out-of-band checks through Family Verify, callback, safe word and the bank registry ([SPEC.md at e6370a5](https://github.com/Galabavamsi/pehchaan/blob/e6370a5/SPEC.md)). A new signal earns a place on site only if it passes three tests. It must be mockable in the engine and demo within hours, have shipped precedent or regulation behind it, and not depend on acoustics. Each new signal goes into SPEC.md first and carries an LR tagged as assumed. It stays out of the simulator's assumptions, so the deck's declared projections do not move.

Guardian co-approval is the strongest addition, and four precedents support it:

| Precedent | What it does |
|---|---|
| Standard Bank Trusted Person (August 2025) | Lets a nominee confirm or decline high-value payments in their own app ([Standard Bank](https://www.standardbank.co.za/southafrica/personal/products-and-services/security-centre/bank-safely/trusted-person)) |
| KBC Guardian Angel | Pauses suspicious payments and alerts both the customer and the guardian; rolling out to more than 4 million customers after a pilot of over 2,000 ([FStech](https://fstech.co.uk/fst/KBC_Rolls_Out_Guardian_Angel_Antifraud_Tool_To_Over_4m_Belgian_Customers.php)) |
| RBI discussion paper | Proposes a trusted person for payments above ₹50,000, mandatory for customers aged 70 and over ([RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?id=23810)) |
| UPI Circle partial delegation | A second person already approves each payment with their own PIN ([Razorpay](https://razorpay.com/blog/what-is-upi-circle/)) |

Family Verify checks the claimed caller, while a guardian checks the payer's intent, so the two are complementary. The guardian step reuses the existing push and signed response, so it takes about half a day. No product publishes an isolated effectiveness figure, so the slide claims precedent, not a number.

CNAP mismatch is the most topical addition. From 1 Nov 2026, a week after the finale, telcos display the KYC-registered caller name by default on 4G and 5G, and label international calls ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/centre-directs-telcos-to-display-caller-names-from-november-1/); [Telangana Today](https://telanganatoday.com/mobile-users-to-see-callers-names-under-new-cnap-framework-from-november-1)). Suppose a caller claims to be the payer's son while CNAP shows an unrelated name, an international label or nothing. That is a moderate signal but never a decisive one. WhatsApp calls carry no CNAP, family SIMs are often registered to another relative, and whether a third-party app can read the CNAP name was not verified.

Screen sharing during a call covers the remote-access and digital-arrest scripts that voice-clone checks miss. Google's India pilot with Google Pay, Paytm and Navi warns users who open a finance app while sharing their screen with an unknown caller, and makes them wait 30 seconds ([Google](https://blog.google/security/android-expands-pilot-in-call-scam-protection-financial-apps/)). Android 16 blocks sideloading and accessibility grants during such calls ([Google Security Blog](https://security.googleblog.com/2025/05/whats-new-in-android-security-privacy-2025.html)).

A human-in-the-loop challenge is the one new signal the team can measure on site. PITCH raised machine AUROC from 56.0% with no challenge to 88.7% with its ten best challenges; humans alone reached 72.6%, and humans plus AI 84.5% ([PITCH](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18085)). DF-CAPTCHA beat passive detectors by 20–29 percentage points ([DF-CAPTCHA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11404)). Both tested older real-time voice conversion, not cloners driven by typed text. An operator typing into an instant-cloning TTS cannot whisper, hum or answer within two seconds. A one-hour experiment with the team's consented voices therefore yields a small, measured, slide-traceable result: challenge the clone pipeline and the real speaker with the same prompts, then record compliance and response delay. The challenge stays an escalation step and is never decisive alone, because stressed genuine callers also fail in noise.

Three tempting additions should stay out. Speaker verification asks whether two recordings share a voice, which is exactly what a clone is built to pass. Open-source clones trained on 20 minutes of speech bypassed an ECAPA-TDNN system at 0.01% false acceptance 43–83% of the time ([arXiv 2601.02914](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02914)). Enrolled voiceprints of relatives would also be biometric data that is itself cloning material.

Watermarks only ever add evidence; their absence proves nothing. The declared Perth test detected the mark in 17 of 57 Chatterbox clips after AMR-NB ([metrics.json at e6370a5](https://github.com/Galabavamsi/pehchaan/blob/e6370a5/ml/results/metrics.json)). Noise followed by generative speech enhancement stripped AudioSeal, WavMark, SilentCipher and Perth to 4% or less ([arXiv 2609.29040](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29040)), and Sarvam's cloning documentation mentions no watermark ([Sarvam](https://docs.sarvam.ai/creative-voice-cloning.md)). `watermark_detected` should stay capped at LR 8, as declared.

Swapping the script-cue model is unjustified without Indian-language call data. Google's on-device Scam Detection in India runs only on Pixel 9 and later, and only in English ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/20/google-steps-up-ai-scam-protection-in-india-but-gaps-remain)). The best small screener's 0.974 AUROC comes from synthetic callers, with acknowledged test-set exposure ([CallScreenBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23959)).

In-band keyed proofs such as CallShield belong on the roadmap, as the fallback when the relative is offline. CallShield reached 99.2% success on clean audio and over 95% under telephony distortion, but needs 63 s per call and the app on both phones ([CallShield](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.09327)).

## India checks numbers and accounts; nobody checks the claim behind the payment

India's national anti-fraud stack is strong at telling whether a number or account is bad:

| System | What it does |
|---|---|
| DoT Financial Fraud Risk Indicator | Rates numbers Medium, High or Very High; PhonePe declines Very High payments. A secondary source reports ₹5,043.73 crore of suspected fraud prevented by August 2026, against an official figure of more than ₹1,000 crore in February ([ET Government](https://government.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/digital-india/5043-crore-saved-how-india-is-building-a-real-time-defence-against-digital-fraud/133989749); [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2223779)) |
| I4C Suspect Registry | Had shared 32.08 lakh mule accounts, with ₹25,698 crore of transactions declined, by 30 June 2026 ([PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2287674)) |
| RBIH MuleHunter.ai | Runs in 26 banks ([RBI Annual Report](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/AnnualReportPublications.aspx?Id=1466)) |
| TRAI 1600 series | Due for commercial banks by 1 January 2026; over 485 BFSI entities held more than 2,800 numbers by November 2025 ([TRAI](https://trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-11/PR_No.135of2025.pdf)) |
| CNAP | Starts on 1 November |

NPCI ended person-to-person collect requests on 1 October 2025, so every P2P payment is now payer-initiated, which is exactly the authorised push payment Pehchaan guards ([The Week/PTI](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/business/2025/08/14/dcm65-biz-npci-collect.html)). The research found no NPCI pause feature and no risk score exposed to apps. None of these checks what a caller claims. A cloned "son" calling from a fresh mule SIM has a clean FRI score, an unknown CNAP name and no 1600 prefix.

RBI's position is split between rules in force and proposals. The 9 April 2026 discussion paper proposes three measures: a one-hour lag on authorised push payments of ₹10,000 or more, a trusted person above ₹50,000 (mandatory at 70 and over), and a kill switch. Comments closed on 8 May, and the research found no draft or final directions by 24 October ([RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?id=23810)).

Two sets of rules are already in force. Section 8 of the Authentication Directions 2025 lets issuers add risk-based checks beyond two-factor authentication from 1 April 2026 ([RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=12898)). The Digital Payment Security Controls Directions of 31 July 2026 require alerts on new beneficiaries and on payees with recorded vishing fraud, according to a published summary ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/rbi/rbi-issues-commercial-banks-digital-payment-security-controls-directions-2026.html)). A bank can therefore deploy GO / PAUSE / HOLD today, with the discussion paper as upside. The paper also leaves open which payments to exempt from the lag, and Pehchaan answers that by adding friction only when a call and a risky payment coincide. RBI has also told the Supreme Court that a liability framework for fraud-induced authorised payments is under consideration ([LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/top-stories/supreme-court-digital-arrest-scam-suo-motu-mha-status-report-sop-rbi-cbi-dot-meity-victim-compensation-liability-framework-522549)). That makes a signed record of what was verified worth money to banks.

Indian payment apps and platforms warn rather than verify. PhonePe's Device Guard warns users who appear to be on a call and behave unusually ([PhonePe](https://www.phonepe.com/blog/trust-and-safety/phonepes-guardrails-advanced-risk-detection-features/)). Google Pay surfaces more than a million warnings a week, and Google's on-device Scam Detection reaches only Pixel 9 and later, under 1% of Indian phones ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/20/google-steps-up-ai-scam-protection-in-india-but-gaps-remain)). SBI activates new internet-banking beneficiaries after an hour and caps transfers to them at ₹5 lakh for the first four days ([SBI](https://onlinesbi.sbi.bank.in/sbijava/new_ben_add_faq_0.html)). Truecaller's AI Call Scanner launched in the US and in English first ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2024/05/223-truecaller-ai-voice-scanner-spam-call/)). Detecting that a user is on a call is no longer novel in India. What is novel is asking who asked, and checking the answer with the real person.

Banks abroad have also shipped pieces of Pehchaan. The closest product analogues verify the bank caller inside the app: Monzo's Call Status ([Android Developers Blog](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2024/03/battling-impersonation-scams-monzo-innovative-approach.html)), CommBank's CallerCheck ([CommBank](https://www.commbank.com.au/articles/newsroom/2023/02/namecheck-callercheck-scam-detection.html)) and Standard Bank's Trust Call ([Standard Bank](https://www.standardbank.co.za/southafrica/personal/products-and-services/security-centre/bank-safely/trust-call)). Pehchaan's 30-minute hold is lighter than any of these hold regimes. Pitch it as the verified first tier that escalates to a bank's longer hold.

The behavioural evidence supports a selective trigger. BioCatch found that fewer than 1% of Android users combine phone calls with mobile banking. More than one in four confirmed fraud cases had the victim on an active call, and an active call is 30 times more common in fraud sessions than in genuine ones. This is 2022 data, mostly from non-Indian banks ([BioCatch/BAI](https://www.biocatch.com/hubfs/BAI-Protecting-Customers-Jan-2022.pdf)).

Safe-word apps depend on the victim remembering to challenge the caller mid-crisis; Signet, for example, rotates a four-word phrase per paired contact ([IzzyOnDroid](https://apt.izzysoft.de/fdroid/index/apk/dev.digitalgrease.signet?repo=main)). Pehchaan instead triggers automatically at Pay and asks the relative's own phone. The table places Pehchaan against each layer.

## Four primary numbers carry the problem slide

India publishes no voice-clone loss figure. The problem slide should therefore combine primary numbers on call-led, authorised, unrecovered fraud with clearly labelled colour.

RBI's 9 April 2026 discussion paper tables NCRP data showing **₹22,931 crore across 28 lakh digital-payment frauds in 2025** (₹22,848 crore and 24 lakh in 2024). It describes these as payments that deceived victims make and approve themselves, and names family impersonation as a common pattern in frauds against vulnerable customers. It also reports that payments above ₹10,000 are about 45% of fraud cases but **98.5% of fraud value** ([RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?id=23810)). That last figure is the case for a payment-time check. Almost all fraud value moves in a few large authorised payments, so checking the risky ones covers it without touching everyday UPI.

DoT's written reply to the Rajya Sabha reports **19,564 Chakshu reports of relative or friend impersonation in 2025**, up from 258 in 2024 and 3.76% of all 5,19,662 Chakshu reports ([PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2223779)). The deck's "76×" ratio should go, for three reasons. The exact ratio is 75.8×, so 76× rounds in the team's favour. Other categories in the same table jumped by similar multiples (investment fraud 176.6×, DoT/TRAI impersonation 71.9×) while total reports grew only 2.5×, which suggests these categories were new in 2024. And Chakshu counts suspected attempts, not losses.

MHA reports more than ₹56,087 crore reported and more than ₹206 crore refunded over FY2023-24 to FY2025-26. That is **0.37%**, not the rounded 0.4% ([PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2287674)). CERT-In's 2024 deepfake advisory names family impersonation and recommends callback procedures for sensitive transactions ([CERT-In](https://www.cert-in.org.in/s2cMainServlet?pageid=PUBVLNOTES02&VLCODE=CIAD-2024-0060)). That is Pehchaan's out-of-band check, in the government's own guidance.

Secondary numbers add the human angle as long as they are labelled. McAfee's 2026 survey reports that 1 in 5 Indians say they have encountered a voice-clone scam, and that the typical scam unfolded in 30 minutes or less. The survey covered 7,592 adults across seven countries and gives no India sample size ([McAfee](https://www.mcafee.com/en-in/consumer-corporate/newsroom/press-releases/2026/20260204-1.html)). A peer-reviewed listening study found clones rated as real as human voices (mean realness 63.0 against 63.8, p = .682). Its listeners were in the UK, and familiar listeners may do better ([PLOS One](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0332692)). The best single case is Indore in January 2026: a teacher paid ₹97,500 in four QR payments in one evening to a caller using her cousin's voice, with a senior police officer on record ([NDTV](https://www.ndtv.com/india-news/scamsters-use-ai-to-clone-cousins-voice-dupe-indore-teacher-of-rs-1-lakh-10604289)). Hyderabad police report ₹102.02 crore lost by senior citizens in 403 cases over 19 months ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/telangana/senior-citizens-lose-102-crore-to-cyber-fraud-in-19-months-in-hyderabad-say-police/article71324536.ece)).

Several numbers should stay off the slides. The RBI Annual Report's 293 card, internet and digital fraud cases in FY26 counts only frauds of ₹1 lakh and above that banks concluded against themselves, not customer-authorised scams ([ETBFSI](https://bfsi.economictimes.indiatimes.com/articles/surge-in-bank-fraud-rs-48021-crore-in-fy26-despite-decrease-in-cases-rbi-reports/131402703)). Counts of 2025 digital-arrest cases conflict. A Supreme Court summary gives 58,249 NCRP complaints ([Drishti IAS](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/supreme-court-directs-measures-to-curb-digital-arrest-scams/print_manually)), MHA's case shares imply about 1.69 lakh, and an op-ed says more than 32,000. The MeitY family-passphrase post should wait until someone opens and checks its source. A vendor chart claiming that 90% of fraud payments happen on an active call has no traceable origin. The slide should say that India does not yet count voice-clone fraud separately, and that Chakshu's relative/friend impersonation category is the closest official proxy.

Every one of these figures also has to trace to a file the repo allows. `engine/pehchaan/sim.py` rewrites `deck_numbers.json` from scratch on each run, so hand-added external figures would vanish the next time the simulator runs. One fix is a small sourced sidecar (value, unit, source URL, access date, primary or secondary) that sim.py merges under an `ext_` prefix. The other is to add the figures after the final simulator run and not re-run it.

## Conclusion

Option 2 costs Pehchaan less than it first appears to. The declared record already holds a sharper honest-negative story than the undeclared one: a detector that looked excellent in simulation failed on real calls, recovered mainly on the handset it was retrained on, and still missed most held-out Chatterbox clones. Its ablation, in which real-call clips plus a learned channel beat either alone, anticipates the main result of RealComm, posted four days before the finale. What the declared record lacks is proof that verification works where detection fails, and that is exactly what two days on site can produce. A timed live hold, a five-person comprehension count and a red-team log are cheaper to produce than a better EER, and they score on more rubric lines.

The larger implication is that India is making claim verification the scarce layer. CNAP and the 1600 series make bank impersonation cheap to check. RBI's proposed trusted person and one-hour lag would add friction to every authorised push payment of ₹10,000 or more unless someone shows which payments can safely skip it. The Supreme Court liability track makes a signed record of what was verified worth money to banks. A product that adds friction only when a call and a risky payment coincide, and resolves it with the real person, fits that trajectory better than any detector. In this pitch, the detector's job is to be measured honestly and to be replaceable.
